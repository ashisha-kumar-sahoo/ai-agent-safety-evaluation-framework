# AI Agent Safety Evaluation Framework

## 1. Introduction

AI agents powered by Large Language Models (LLMs) can reason, make decisions, use tools, and perform multi-step tasks. As these agents become more autonomous, ensuring their safety, reliability, and responsible behavior becomes important.

Our project focuses on studying existing approaches for evaluating the safety of AI agents and identifying the research gap for developing an **AI Agent Safety Evaluation Framework**.

## 2. Objectives

- Study existing AI agent safety and evaluation approaches.
- Identify major safety risks in AI agents.
- Analyze existing safety frameworks and evaluation methods.
- Identify limitations and research gaps.
- Define important criteria for evaluating AI agent safety.

## 3. Literature Survey

We studied four research papers related to AI agent safety and evaluation.

### Paper 1: Enterprise Agent Evaluation Framework

The paper proposes a multi-dimensional framework for evaluating autonomous AI agents. It evaluates areas such as reasoning accuracy, hallucination, tool utilization, workflow completion, security, reliability, cost, and human satisfaction. It also introduces the Enterprise Agent Score (EAS).

### Paper 2: AI Agents: Architecture, Safety Frameworks, and Emerging Applications

This paper studies AI agent architectures and major safety concerns such as alignment, robustness, interpretability, controllability, and societal safety. It proposes a four-layer safety framework covering objective alignment, behavioral constraints, monitoring and auditability, and societal governance.

### Paper 3: OPENAGENTSAFETY

This paper presents a framework for evaluating AI agent safety in realistic environments. It contains 350+ executable tasks covering different safety risks and uses tools such as file systems, Python, web browsers, and Unix shells.

### Paper 4: Advancing Innovation in Financial Stability

This paper reviews AI agent frameworks such as LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen, and LangChain. It discusses challenges including reliability, explainability, safety, regulatory compliance, risk alignment, and scalability.

## 4. Key Findings

From our literature survey, we found that AI agent safety should consider multiple factors rather than only checking the accuracy of the final answer.

Important areas identified are:

- Goal / Objective Alignment
- Security
- Privacy
- Hallucination
- Robustness
- Tool Usage
- Reliability
- Harmful Actions
- Human Oversight
- Multi-Agent Behavior

## 5. Research Gap

Existing research provides different approaches for AI agent safety and evaluation. Some focus on multi-dimensional evaluation, while others focus on realistic safety testing, safety frameworks, or agent architectures.

Our study indicates a need for a structured evaluation approach that brings important safety criteria together for evaluating AI agents comprehensively. Existing research also identifies the lack of standardized and holistic evaluation methods for autonomous AI agents.
